# Porch Supporter — Phase 13 content collection

Prepared October 24, 2026. Public payments remain closed.

## Pull Up a Chair

Seven reflections, in any order. Skip a prompt if you prefer. An unfinished reflection can be saved as a draft; marking it complete requires all three answers. Nothing is saved until Save reflection is pressed. Private saving requires approved access. All prompts and the separate Journal remain free.

The existing 21 prompts are unchanged so previously saved answers retain their meaning. These are reflection prompts, not a diagnostic assessment. No responses are used in the card designs.

### 1. Start where you are

You do not have to have everything figured out to take one useful step.

1. What feels heaviest today?
2. What is one thing you can make a little easier?
3. What would enough look like for today?

One small step: Choose one small task that is within reach.

### 2. Remember your why

Your reasons can change. They can be ordinary, personal, and entirely yours.

1. What do you want more room for in your life?
2. What has recovery already made possible, however small?
3. What would you like to remind yourself on a difficult day?

One small step: Write one sentence you can come back to.

### 3. Notice the hard moments

Noticing a pattern is information, not a verdict on you.

1. When does the day tend to get harder?
2. What do you notice in your body or surroundings then?
3. What has helped you pause before?

One small step: Choose a place, activity, or person that can help with a pause.

### 4. Make room for support

Support can come from different people, communities, and practical resources.

1. Who helps you feel heard?
2. What kind of help would actually be useful?
3. What is one small way you could ask for it?

One small step: Draft a simple request for support, without pressure to send it.

### 5. Build a gentler day

A useful routine supports your life. It does not have to fill every hour.

1. What part of your day needs a little more care?
2. Which basic need have you been putting off?
3. What is one routine you could make smaller and easier?

One small step: Choose one manageable act of care for today.

### 6. Leave room to begin again

A difficult day does not erase the work you have done.

1. What would you say to a friend in your situation?
2. What can you learn without turning it into blame?
3. What would help with your next safe step?

One small step: Write a kind, practical next-step reminder.

### 7. Carry something forward

Progress can be quiet. You are allowed to notice it.

1. What have you learned about what helps you?
2. Which small change would you like to keep?
3. What deserves a little credit today?

One small step: Choose one thing to carry into the coming week.
